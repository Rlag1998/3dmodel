How the model works

  • Each person carries two copies of 22 autosomes plus X and Y. Loci sit at approximate GRCh38 positions, so genes that are close together, like HERC2 and OCA2, tend to travel together.
  • Eggs and sperm are made by meiosis with Poisson crossovers at sex-specific rates: about 1.55 cM/Mb in mothers and 0.9 cM/Mb in fathers. Sons get their only X, and so their AR baldness allele, from their mother.
  • Named loci are real genes with published associations. Effect sizes are illustrative and tuned by hand. Loci tagged unmapped stand in for the many small-effect variants behind traits like face shape.

What it simplifies

  • Dimples, cleft chin and widow's peak use the dominant model from school biology. Real inheritance of these traits is murkier.
  • Gene pool frequencies are rough. Real populations vary a great deal within every region, and morphology loci use the same frequencies everywhere.
  • The mutation rate is exaggerated so you can see it happen. Real rates are around one in a hundred million per base per generation.
